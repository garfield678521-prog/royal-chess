♟ Chess Rules & Move Types

Objective

The objective is to checkmate the opposing king. Checkmate happens when a king is in check and has no legal way to escape.

♔ King

The king moves one square in any direction. The king may never move onto a square attacked by an enemy piece.

♕ Queen

The queen can move any number of squares horizontally, vertically, or diagonally.

♖ Rook

The rook moves any number of unobstructed squares horizontally or vertically.

♗ Bishop

The bishop moves any number of unobstructed squares diagonally. Each bishop stays on the same colour square for the entire game.

♘ Knight

The knight moves in an L shape: two squares in one direction and one square perpendicular. Knights can jump over pieces.

♙ Pawn

Pawns normally move one square forward. From their starting position they may move two squares. Pawns capture one square diagonally forward.

🏰 Castling

Castling moves the king two squares toward a rook and moves that rook to the square next to the king.

Castling is only legal when:

  • The king has not moved.
  • The rook involved has not moved.
  • The squares between them are empty.
  • The king is not currently in check.
  • The king does not cross an attacked square.
  • The king does not finish on an attacked square.

🎯 En Passant

If an enemy pawn moves two squares forward from its starting position and lands beside your pawn, your pawn may capture it as though it had moved only one square. This must be done immediately on the next move.

👑 Promotion

When a pawn reaches the opposite end of the board it must be promoted to a:

  • Queen
  • Rook
  • Bishop
  • Knight

⚔ Check

A king is in check when an enemy piece attacks its square. The player must remove the check before making another move.

💀 Checkmate

Checkmate occurs when the king is in check and there is no legal move that can remove the check.

🤝 Stalemate

Stalemate occurs when the player whose turn it is has no legal moves but their king is not in check. The game is a draw.

🔁 Threefold Repetition

If the same position occurs three times, a draw can be claimed.

50-Move Rule

If 50 moves by each player occur without a pawn move or capture, a draw can be claimed.

♔♚ Insufficient Material

If neither player has enough material to possibly checkmate, the game is drawn.

📝 Chess Notation

Moves use standard algebraic notation such as:

  • e4 — pawn moves to e4
  • Nf3 — knight moves to f3
  • Bxc4 — bishop captures on c4
  • O-O — kingside castling
  • O-O-O — queenside castling
  • e8=Q — pawn promotes to queen
  • + — check
  • # — checkmate
